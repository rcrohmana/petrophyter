# Petrophyter v1.13.0 (Build 20261106)

Petrophyter v1.13.0 adds variable cementation and saturation exponents and a radial invasion correction of Rt to the LRLC Workbench. Both are optional: **results are unchanged unless you switch Variable m/n to curve mode or add the invasion step to a zone's Rt corrections.** One correction to an existing result is listed under Fixes.

## New

- **Variable m and n.** In curve mode each sample gets its own cementation exponent `M_VAR` and saturation exponent `N_VAR`, and every Sw model reads them instead of the single zone values.
  - m comes from a formula in porosity and Vsh (`m = c0 + c1·PHI + c2·Vsh + c3/PHI`) or from the Shell formula for carbonates. The Shell formula is evaluated only in zones whose lithology preset is Carbonate unless you confirm it for the zone.
  - n is the zone constant or a linear function of Vsh.
  - Values are clipped to an editable range (m 1 to 4, n 1 to 4 by default), and the number clipped is reported after the run. Samples where the formula cannot be evaluated keep the zone m and n and are counted.
  - With the default coefficients (`c0` equal to the zone m) the curve is a constant, and nothing changes.
- **Variable-m fit.** **Fit m to the wet points** fits the formula coefficients to the water-bearing points of the Pickett plot, using the Rt that the Sw models read and the zone's Rw. It is robust to outliers, drops the coefficients the data cannot support, and shows standard errors and an m-versus-porosity plot. It stays a proposal until you apply it.
- **Radial invasion correction.** The `invasion` step of the Rt chain fits a step invasion profile (flushed zone of diameter `di` and resistivity `Rxo`, then the undisturbed formation) to the deep, medium and shallow resistivity. It writes `RT_INV`, `RXO_INV` and `DI_INV`.
  - Tool presets give the investigation diameters and shape of a generic induction or laterolog response. They are generic, not vendor specifications, and the panel says so; custom diameters are accepted.
  - Samples where the curves barely differ (non-unique), where the invasion diameter hits a bound, or where the fit is poor are counted and reported. A flushed-zone resistivity below Rmf is flagged.
  - With a mapped RXO curve, Rxo can be fixed to it.
  - The step needs RM and RS (and optionally RXO) already corrected for borehole, mud and shoulder-bed effects. The panel says this plainly.
- **Rmf at formation temperature.** Rmf is brought to formation temperature with the Arps relation. The LAS header's mud filtrate resistivity and its temperature are offered as a proposal, written only when you accept it. The run warns when the temperature setup falls back to a default.
- **Workbench Remediation sections** for Variable m/n (settings, Shell badge, fit and plot, last-run percentiles and counts) and Radial invasion (tool, diameters, Rmf, counts per zone, a profile at a chosen depth, and the RS/RT versus RM/RT crossplot with forward loci). Both have a Method & Reference box.
- **Methods panel and compact Method & Reference strips.** The LRLC Workbench has a fifth panel, **Methods**, with the full write-up of every method: typeset equations, a table explaining each symbol, the assumptions and limits, and the references with DOI links. The Method & Reference boxes under the tools are now a compact strip (title, equations, short citations and a link that opens the Methods panel at that method).
- **Remediation panel reorganised.** The Rt source and the zone/Apply header stay at the top, and each correction (Rt chain, laminated sand, pyrite, clay Qv, Sw basis, variable m/n, radial invasion) has its own inner tab with compact inputs and its plots beside the settings. Menu entries and issue links open the matching tab.
- **Specialized → Saturation Tools → Variable m/n…** and **Specialized → Rt Corrections → Radial Invasion Correction…** open the matching section.
- **Log display.** `RT_INV` (solid) and `RXO_INV` (dotted) are drawn on the resistivity track in both log engines. `M_VAR`, `N_VAR` and `DI_INV` appear in the crosshair readout.
- **Monte Carlo.** In curve mode, m and n are varied as offsets of the whole curve; the clipped range still applies. The invasion step is held at its base result and listed as not varied.
- **Exports.** The new columns appear in CSV, Excel and LAS, with LAS units and curve descriptions. Provenance adds a "Variable m/n" line when a zone uses curve mode and an "Invasion tool" line when the step ran.

## Sessions

- A session is saved as format **2.7** only when a zone uses curve mode, a zone runs the invasion step, or a variable m/n or invasion setting differs from its default. Any other session is saved as 2.6 or older as before.
- **A 2.7 session opens in v1.12.0** with a notice that settings it does not know were not loaded. There the exponents are the zone constants and the invasion step is dropped, so Sw and pay can differ from v1.13.0.

## Fixes

- The Rt cutoff (`RT_CUT`) now uses exactly the per-sample inputs of the primary Sw model. In v1.12.0 it used the bulk porosity, bulk Vsh and the single Waxman-Smits Qv even in zones whose Sw read the sand-lamina basis or an estimated Qv. In those zones `RT_CUT` and the flags derived from it can change. Zones without these features are unaffected.

## Calculation changes

- None to existing results, apart from the Rt cutoff fix above. Every reference case is unchanged relative to v1.12.0. Two new reference cases cover a shaly sand with variable exponents in all five Sw models and a synthetic invaded well, where the invasion step recovers the known Rt, Rxo and invasion diameter.

## Known issues

- The invasion model is a step profile only: no annulus, transition zone or flushing gradient. It is not a substitute for the service company's tool-specific charts, and it should not be applied to resistivities that have already been invasion-corrected.
- The generic tool presets are design placeholders. Confirm the diameters and shape against the chart book of the tool that was run.
- The Shell formula raises m as porosity falls. Borai (1987) found the opposite trend in tight carbonates offshore Abu Dhabi, so check it against core before relying on it.
- The variable-m fit depends on Rw. With an automatic Rw, estimated with the constant m, the fit is circular, and the panel notes this.
- There is no fit for n.
- The Pickett plot still draws its iso-saturation lines with the constant zone m, also in zones that use curve mode.
- The references "Tolioe et al. (2016)" and "Guo et al. (2023)" are still marked "to be confirmed".

## Installation

1. Download `Petrophyter_Setup_1.13.0_Build20261106.exe` from **Assets**.
2. Optionally verify it using `SHA256SUMS.txt`:

   ```powershell
   Get-FileHash .\Petrophyter_Setup_1.13.0_Build20261106.exe -Algorithm SHA256
   ```

3. Run the installer and launch **Petrophyter** from the Start Menu. Installing over v1.12.0 upgrades it in place.

> Windows SmartScreen may appear because the installer is unsigned. Select **More info → Run anyway** only after confirming the SHA-256 hash.
