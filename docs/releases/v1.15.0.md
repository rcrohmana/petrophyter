# Petrophyter v1.15.0 (Build 20261115)

Petrophyter v1.15.0 is a clean-up release. Where a number could be wrong without a warning, the run now says so. A few long-standing items are fixed, and the limits of several tools are stated at the controls. **Two calculations change** (see Calculation changes): the laminated-zone check of the shoulder-bed guard, and the Doll response of the Rt deconvolution. Everything else adds messages and text, and existing results stay the same.

## New

- **Warnings where a number could be wrong.**
  - **Deviated well without TVD.** The LAS header is now read for deviation entries (`MDEV`, `MAXDEV`, `HDEV`, `DEVI`, `DEV`, `INCL`, and `MHD` when its unit is degrees). An inclination curve (`DEVI`, `DEV`, `INCL`, `HDEV`, `DEVIATION`, `INCLINATION`) counts as well. When either reads 5 degrees or more and no TVD curve is mapped, the saturation-height and temperature notes become warnings, for example *Curve DEVI reads up to 35 degrees, so the well looks deviated, but no TVD curve is mapped; heights assume a vertical well. Map a TVD curve in Curve Mapping.* A vertical well keeps the info line *Height computed on MD (assumed vertical).*
  - **Saturation-height pay with a permeability from the resistivity Sw.** When saturation-height Sw is used for pay and the permeability is `PERM_WR` or `PERM_TIMUR`, which come from the resistivity Sw through Swirr, the run warns that the pay Sw is not independent of resistivity. The same applies to `PERM_RT` when some rock types come from the logs. Pay is unchanged. The stats note on the tab names the permeability.
  - **Built-in fluid values.** While the fluid densities and interfacial tension are still the built-in values and have not been confirmed, the run says so. It is a warning when saturation-height Sw is used for pay, and an info line otherwise. **Confirm** on the Saturation-Height tab is now saved with the session. The summary, the stats note and the export show the interfacial tension used, and the export has new **Fluid density** and **Sigma cos** columns.
  - **Thin beds in the deconvolution.** For each zone where the deconvolution ran, the run counts the beds thinner than the response width, for example *Deconvolution: 2 of 6 beds in Zone LOWER are thinner than the response width (4.0 ft), 10 % of the bed thickness. Thin beds are only partly recovered: Rt stays low there and Sw is too high.* It is an info line, and a warning when those beds are a quarter or more of the bed thickness or more than half of the beds.
  - **Core porosity scale.** When a well's core porosity is read as fractions from its median, but some values are above 1, the import notes and the load banner say how many were excluded and suggest setting the scale to Percent. The same applies to capillary-pressure files.
- **The mapped RT can be marked invasion corrected.** A new check on the Overview page of the LRLC Workbench, **The mapped RT is invasion corrected**, sits under the shoulder-bed one. With the guard on, the radial invasion step is then left out in every zone, because it needs the raw deep RT. With the guard off, it runs with a warning.
- **Limits stated at the controls.**
  - The laminated-sand step uses one Rsh for the whole zone and does not model shale anisotropy.
  - The default shale PEF of 3.42 b/e is an average.
  - The Diagnosis thresholds are design defaults.
  - The Shell badge in a carbonate zone notes that Borai (1987) found the opposite trend in tight carbonates.
- **Session file name in the window title**, for example `mysession.json — BKS-02 · 2 wells — Petrophyter`. Before a session is saved, the title is as before.
- **The multi-well import dialog fits the screen.** It opens at its usual size, made smaller when the screen is smaller.
- **Fuller Uncertainty texts.**
  - The tab lists everything that is not varied: the porosity inputs (densities, shale point, NPHI matrix, gas correction), the GR baseline, temperature and depth.
  - It says that one draw applies to every well and zone.
  - The tornado chart is titled *One parameter at a time, the others at base*.

## Calculation changes

- **The laminated-zone check leaves out resolved shale beds.**
  - **What changed.** Shale beds at least one response width thick are left out of the laminated-zone check, together with 3 ft on either side. Here a shale bed means samples at or above the Thomas-Stieber shale Vsh, and the response width is the deconvolution width setting, also when the deconvolution is off. A zone with less than a tenth of its thickness left for the check is not classed laminated.
  - **What this changes for you.** A zone of interbedded sand and shale that the logs resolve is no longer classed laminated, so the guard no longer leaves the deconvolution out there. The guard hint and the Diagnosis follow the same check. The Overview guard line says when it changed the class, for example *Zone UPPER: not laminated (10 ft of resolved shale beds left out of the laminated check).*
  - **Which wells.** Only wells with Thomas-Stieber end-points applied are affected. Sw and pay change only in zones where a step the guard used to drop now runs. Laminations thinner than the response width still count as laminated.
- **The Doll response of the deconvolution is corrected.**
  - It is now the closed form of the two-coil geometric factor: flat between the coils and falling with the square of the distance outside. It is checked against the numerical integral of the geometric factor; the original paper was not consulted.
  - Results change for anyone who chose the Doll response. The Gaussian response, the default, is unchanged.
- **Nothing else changes a result.** Every other change in this version adds a message or text. The existing reference cases keep their results; three of them gain the new messages.

## Sessions and out-of-date wells

- A session is saved as format **2.9** only when a well has its mapped RT marked invasion corrected. Every other session is saved as 2.8 or older, as before.
- **A 2.9 session opens in v1.14.0** with a notice that settings it does not know were not loaded. The mark is one of them, so v1.14.0 applies the radial invasion step to that RT again.
- The fluid confirmation is saved only once you confirm, and the new guard settings only when they differ from their defaults. A session that uses neither is written exactly as before.
- No well shows as out of date because of the upgrade. Results are never stored in a session, so after loading, run the wells as usual (**Analysis → Run All Wells**).

## Fixes

- The deviation warning described in the user guide can now appear: the LAS parser reads deviation entries and inclination curves.
- A deconvolution proposal for one zone shows that zone's own bed count and messages, without those of other zones where the step is stored.
- The Methods text and the documents are brought up to date: the Rt deconvolution ships, Guo et al. (2023) is confirmed, and the header Rmf is read by the invasion page.

## Known issues

**Depth and deviation**
- A TVD computed from a deviation survey or an inclination curve is not supported; map a TVD curve for a deviated well.

**Saturation height**
- The free water level is one depth per well or zone. There is no pressure-gradient free water level and no closure correction beyond the minimum Pc you set.
- When the rock-type definitions are fitted per zone, or rock typing is off, one fit is used for all samples.
- Log-derived fits are a cross-check only; they cannot be used for pay.

**Uncertainty**
- Porosity inputs, the GR baseline, temperature and depth are not varied, so the spread is understated. Where saturation-height Sw is used for pay, it is held at its value.
- One draw applies to every well and zone; per-well distributions are not available.
- HCPV is a thickness per unit area in ft, not a volume.
- The tornado changes one parameter at a time and misses interactions.

**Rock typing**
- Rock types in uncored intervals inherit the shape of the Timur or Wyllie-Rose permeability.
- Winland R35 was derived on sandstones and carries no warning in carbonate zones.
- Core porosity above the rock-typing range (0.50) still enters the permeability fit.

**LRLC corrections**
- Thomas-Stieber uses one pair of end-points per zone and assumes PHIT is free of shale and gas effects; in gas zones it gives only an info line.
- The laminated check of the guard still has gaps:
  - Shale beds read through noise or below the Thomas-Stieber shale Vsh stay laminated.
  - Isolated shale beds of 3 to 5 ft in clean sand stay laminated.
  - Up to 3 ft next to a resolved shale bed is left out of the check.
  - The check was tested on synthetic wells only.
- The invasion model is a step profile only, and its generic tool presets are placeholders to confirm against the tool charts.
- The variable-m fit depends on Rw and is circular with an automatic Rw. There is no fit for `n`.
- The Pickett plot draws its iso-Sw lines with the constant m when several zones are plotted and some use curve mode.
- Manual wet-point boxes on the Pickett plot are not saved with the session.
- The screening, diagnosis and elastic thresholds are design defaults to calibrate per field.
- The hydrocarbon criterion needs the gas flag or a shallow curve, and deep reading at or above shallow is a weak indicator with saline mud.
- Flushed-zone curves are masked for values at or below zero at load only when they are declared in ohm units.

**Deconvolution**
- The tool presets are generic widths, not vendor specifications.
- Array induction curves already carry their own vertical enhancement, so the deconvolution enhances them twice, and nothing detects it.
- The deconvolution needs a reference curve that resolves the beds.
- Beds thinner than the response width are only partly recovered (the run now counts them).

**Elastic flag**
- It reads sonic and density, which see the flushed zone. Gassmann fluid substitution is not modelled.

**References**
- These entries are still marked "to verify". Six are cited by the method texts of shipped tools: Simandoux (1963), Poupon and Leveaux (1971), Morris and Biggs (1967), Crain's Petrophysical Handbook, Rasmus (1986), and Leverett (1941), on which the J-function of the saturation-height tool rests.
- The others are in the reference list but not cited by a method text: Tolioe et al. (2016), Gao et al. (2022), IPA (1998), ONGC (2025), Skelt and Harrison (1995), Harrison and Jing (2001), and Tudge (2010).

**Installer**
- The installer has not been tried on a PC without Anaconda for v1.14.0 or v1.15.0.
- The Interactive log engine is checked on axes and items in automated tests; its drawing is checked by hand.

## Installation

1. Download `Petrophyter_Setup_1.15.0_Build20261115.exe` from **Assets**.
2. Optionally verify it using `SHA256SUMS.txt`:

   ```powershell
   Get-FileHash .\Petrophyter_Setup_1.15.0_Build20261115.exe -Algorithm SHA256
   ```

3. Run the installer and launch **Petrophyter** from the Start Menu. Installing over v1.14.0 upgrades it in place.

> Windows SmartScreen may appear because the installer is unsigned. Select **More info → Run anyway** only after confirming the SHA-256 hash.
